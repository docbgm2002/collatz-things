WHY THIS GAME EXISTS

Understand a rule before trusting a result.

Collatz asks whether repeatedly applying its rule eventually reaches 1 for every positive integer. Playing a finite set of journeys cannot prove that. It can help us learn, spot loops in other rules, and share experiments someone else can check.

FROM THE CORRECTION TO THE GAME

Our merged correction keeps the accumulated “+1” terms in the arithmetic. The ship follows the complete integer calculation at every move. That is why this game uses exact integers, even when the display rounds a ring label.

WELCOME, EXPLORER

One ship. One number.
One surprisingly tricky rule.

  1. Press Play. The ship carries the current number. Every move applies the rule shown beside it.
  2. Watch the arithmetic. For the classic rule, even numbers halve; odd numbers become 3 times the number, plus 1.
  3. Try predicting a move. Pause, choose inward, outward, or same, and see the calculation.
  4. Change the rule. Some experiments reach 1. Others loop. A limit means we stopped looking, not that a journey can never end.
  5. Save and compare. Keep the rule and full calculation history. Export or import a replay to check it again.

This teaches mathematical iteration and loops. It does not simulate gravity, explain galaxies, or prove the Collatz conjecture.
