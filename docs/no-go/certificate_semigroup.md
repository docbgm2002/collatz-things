W2 asked: replace "sum of certified numbers" by "orbit of certified numbers
under certificate-preserving maps", and characterize \(S\cdot B\) for
\(B=\mathrm{FIN1}=\{x\text{ odd}:x\le10^6\}\).

The answer is a dichotomy with nothing in between.

> **W2-KILL.** Sort the repo's proved transport rules by the mechanism of
> the proof. Every one falls into exactly one of three classes:
>
> 1. **forward-orbit rules** (the Mersenne burn, the mod-8 rail identities)
>    — *void*: they enlarge a finite certified set only finitely, because
>    the "reaches 1" set is closed under the map by definition;
> 2. **named one-parameter rules** (\(\sigma:n\mapsto4n+1\); the

>    \(\Theta((\log X)^2)\), natural density \(0\), and countable
>    (hence nowhere dense, measure-zero) \(2\)-adic closure;
> 3. **the unrestricted predecessor selectors** \(P_e\), \(e\ge1\) — in
>    which case \(S\cdot B=\{n:n\text{ reaches }1\}\) verbatim, because
>    \(1\in B\) and the \(P_e\) generate the full backward Collatz tree.
>    Characterizing \(S\cdot B\) *is* the conjecture.
>
> There is no proper sub-semigroup in the repo's inventory that is both
> compositional and non-thin. The task's nominated escape hatch — "the burn
> moves between itinerary cylinders" — fails, because the burn is in class 1.

The task's own kill rule was "kill if every proved transport rule is
parity-word-preserving on a bounded window, since then the orbit is a

compose**. \(\sigma\) and \(\delta\) are the only rules that iterate

§4.5), and their joint orbit is a two-dimensional lattice triangle — hence

## 1. Deliverable (i): what counts as a transport rule

**Definition (transport rule).** A *transport rule* is a partial map
\(g:\mathcal D_g\to\mathbb Z_{>0}\), \(\mathcal D_g\subseteq\mathbb Z_{>0}\),
together with a proof of

\[
n\in\mathcal D_g \ \text{ and }\ n\text{ reaches }1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
g(n)\text{ reaches }1 .
\tag{T}
\]

Write \(\mathcal O^+(n)=\{n,C(n),C^2(n),\dots\}\) for the forward orbit.

**Definition (void rule).** A transport rule is **void** if
\(g(n)\in\mathcal O^+(n)\) for every \(n\in\mathcal D_g\).

> **Lemma 1 (void rules are worthless).** Let \(B\) be finite with every
> \(b\in B\) reaching \(1\), and let \(S\) be generated by void rules alone.
> Then \(S\cdot B\subseteq\bigcup_{b\in B}\mathcal O^+(b)\), a **finite**
> set.

*Proof.* Each \(\mathcal O^+(b)\) is finite because \(b\) reaches \(1\) and
\(\mathcal O^+(1)=\{1,2,4\}\); a finite union of finite sets is finite.
\(\square\)

This is the triviality the definition must exclude, and it is not a
technicality: it removes three of the repo's five nominated generators.
The verifier reports, for instance, that the forward orbits of all odd
\(b<20001\) contain only \(15\,876\) distinct odd values in total (largest
\(9\,038\,141\)).

**Definition (merge depth).** A non-void rule has *merge depth* \((a,b)\) if
\(f^{a}(g(n))=f^{b}(n)\) identically on \(\mathcal D_g\), with \(a\) minimal.
Rules of depth \((a,0)\) are **predecessor rules**; depth \((a,b)\) with
\(b\ge1\) are **sibling rules**.

The certification power of a rule is entirely in the depth-\((a,b)\)
identity: (T) holds because the two trajectories literally merge, so
\(g(n)\)'s tail is \(n\)'s tail. Nothing else in the repo proves (T).

## 3. Deliverable (iv): the new generator, and what TWR1 really is

The search asked for one *new* generator from the repo's exact identities.
The find is not a new map but a new identification.

> **W2-A (proved here).** Let \(P_2(z)=(4z-1)/3\). Then
> \[
> P_2^{\,d}(z)=1+\Bigl(\tfrac43\Bigr)^{d}(z-1),
> \]
> defined (as an integer) exactly when \(3^{d}\mid z-1\). Specialising to
> \(z=2^{M}-1\) gives
> \[
> P_2^{\,d}(2^M-1)=1+2^{\,2d+1}\frac{2^{M-1}-1}{3^{d}}=w_d(M),
> \]
> and the domain condition \(3^d\mid2^{M}-2\) is equivalent to
> \(M\equiv1\pmod{2\cdot3^{d-1}}\).

*Proof.* \(P_2\) is the affine map \(z\mapsto\frac43z-\frac13\) with fixed
point \(1\), so \(P_2(z)-1=\frac43(z-1)\) and the iterate is immediate;
integrality is \(3^d\mid z-1\). For the specialisation,
\(2^M-2=2(2^{M-1}-1)\) and \(3\nmid2\), so \(3^d\mid2^{M-1}-1\), i.e.
\(\mathrm{ord}_{3^d}(2)=2\cdot3^{d-1}\) divides \(M-1\) (2 is a primitive
root mod \(3^d\)). Comparing with the TWR1 formula
\(w_d(M)=1+2^{2d+1}(2^{M-1}-1)/3^d\) finishes. \(\square\)

So **the Mersenne ancestry tower is precisely the single \(e{=}2\)
predecessor selector, iterated** — every tower step is a step with
\(v_2(3w_d+1)=2\) (T1(b)), and the "conjectured period \(2,6,18,\dots\)"
domain condition is nothing but the shrinking integrality condition
\(3^d\mid z-1\) of \(P_2^d\). This is a genuine simplification of TWR1 and
is the closest thing to a new generator the inventory yields.

It also settles the classification question immediately: \(P_2\) is a
predecessor rule of depth \((1,0)\), it moves between itinerary cylinders
(it prepends the letter \(e=2\)), and it is not parity-word-preserving on a
bounded window in the sense of the task's kill rule. The kill therefore
cannot come from barrier 1. It comes from counting.

## 4. Deliverable (iii): the orbit, closed completely

### 4.1 Structure

\(\sigma\) maps odds to odds with image exactly \(\{x\equiv5\ (8)\}\), so
each odd \(y\) has a unique *primitive* \(\sigma\)-root: strip
\(\sigma^{-1}(y)=(y-1)/4\) while \(y\equiv5\ (8)\). Hence every element of
\(\langle\sigma,\delta\rangle\cdot B\) has a **unique** representation
\(2^{m}\sigma^{k}(b)\) with \(b\in B'\), where

\[
B'=\{b\in B:\ b\not\equiv5\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 8)\},\qquad |B'|=\tfrac34|B| .
\]

For \(B=\mathrm{FIN1}\): \(|B|=500\,001\), \(|B'|=375\,001\).

### 4.2 Counting function (exact)

\(\sigma^k(b)=\frac{4^k(3b+1)-1}{3}=4^kb\,(1+O(1/b))\), so the admissible
pairs \((k,m)\) form the lattice triangle \(\{m+2k\le u_b\}\),
\(u_b=\log_2(X/b)\).

> **W2-B (proved here; exact count verified).**
> \[
> \bigl|\langle\sigma,\delta\rangle\cdot B\cap[1,X]\bigr|
> =\sum_{b\in B'}\#\{(k,m)\ge0:2^m\sigma^k(b)\le X\}
> =\frac14\sum_{b\in B'}\bigl(\log_2\tfrac Xb+2\bigr)^2+O(|B'|)
> \]
> \[
> \sim\ \frac{|B'|}{4}\,(\log_2X)^2 .
> \]
> In particular the natural density is \(0\).

Measured against the triangle law (verifier, \(B=\mathrm{FIN1}\)):

| \(\log_2X\) | exact count | triangle law | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 21 389 705 | 22 750 991 | 0.940 |
| 48 | 90 535 609 | 93 284 567 | 0.971 |
| 64 | 207 681 641 | 211 818 270 | 0.981 |
| 96 | 585 974 089 | 592 886 061 | 0.988 |
| 128 | 1 156 267 049 | 1 165 954 363 | 0.992 |

The crude law \(\frac{|B'|}{4}(\log_2X)^2\) is a strict upper bound at every
scale, approached from below. (The verifier also confirms the analytic count
against explicit enumeration, i.e. that the representation really is
collision-free.)

### 4.3 \(2\)-adic closure

In \(\mathbb Z_2\), \(\sigma^k(b)=4^k\bigl(b+\tfrac13\bigr)-\tfrac13\), and
\(4^k\to0\), so

\[
\sigma^k(b)\ \longrightarrow\ -\tfrac13=1+4+16+\cdots=(\dots010101)_2 .
\]

> **W2-C.** The \(2\)-adic closure of \(\langle\sigma\rangle\cdot B\) is that
> set together with the single point \(-\frac13\); adjoining \(\delta\) adds
> only the limit \(0\). The closure is **countable**, hence nowhere dense
> and of Haar measure zero.

This is strictly stronger than the outcome W2 hoped to file ("if \(S\cdot B\)
is contained in a proper \(2\)-adic congruence class union, that is an exact
no-go"). It is not merely inside a congruence union — it is inside a
countable set. No certificate-transport proof of this shape can reach a
positive-measure set of \(2\)-adic exponents, let alone all of them.

The limit point is worth a remark: \(-\frac13\) is the \(2\)-adic *ghost* of
the \(\sigma\)-tower, the exact analogue of the ghost
\(3^{\alpha}=-7\) that governs BAKER1–3 (see
`baker_explicit_constants_audit.md` §4). In both cases the finite family is
a sequence of integer truncations of one irrational \(2\)-adic point, and in
both cases the truncations grow geometrically — which is why both families
are thin.

### 4.4 Intersection with the repunit family

\(a_n=\sigma^k(b)\) is equivalent to \(2^{2k+1}(3b+1)=3^{n+1}-1\), so
\(2k+1\le v_2(3^{n+1}-1)=2+v_2(n+1)\), giving the elementary cap
\(4^k\le4(n+1)\) and hence \(a_n\le4(n+1)\cdot\max B\). For
\(B=\mathrm{FIN1}\) this forces \(n\le17\), and the complete solution list is

\[
(n,k,b)\in\{(1,0,1),(3,0,13),(3,1,3),(5,0,121),(7,0,1093),(7,1,273),
\]
\[
(9,0,9841),(11,0,88573),(11,1,22143),(13,0,797161),(15,2,448403)\}.
\]

> **W2-D.** \(\langle\sigma,\delta\rangle\cdot\mathrm{FIN1}\) certifies no
> repunit beyond \(a_{15}=7\,174\,453\). The open range of the repunit
> programme (\(n\) in the hundreds and thousands) is never reached.

There is one structural fact here worth keeping:

> **W2-E.** \(a_n\) lies in the image of \(\sigma\) **iff** \(n\equiv3\ (4)\),
> and then \(\sigma^{-1}(a_n)=(3^n-3)/8\).

So half the repunit family always has a \(\sigma\)-parent — but the parent
is \(\approx a_n/4\), still of the same exponential size. \(\sigma\) moves
*down* the repunit family by a bounded factor, never out of it. That is the
repunit-local face of the thinness in §4.2.

### 4.5 The \(P_2\) orbit is finite

By W2-A, \(P_2^d(b)\) exists only for \(d\le v_3(b-1)\). So the depth budget
of a seed is a fixed \(3\)-adic valuation, not a free parameter:

> **W2-F.** \(|\langle P_2\rangle\cdot B|\le\sum_{b\in B}(v_3(b-1)+1)\), a
> finite number. For \(B=\mathrm{FIN1}\) it is at most \(749\,993\), with
> maximum usable depth \(d=11\).

The tower looks unbounded only because its seed \(2^M-1\) is unbounded — and
that seed must already be certified before the tower can be applied. TWR1
transports certificates *within* the Mersenne family; it does not
manufacture them.

## 5. The dichotomy, stated exactly

> **W2-G.** Let \(\mathcal P=\{P_e:e\ge1\}\) be the full set of depth-\(1\)
> predecessor rules and \(B\ni1\). Then
> \(\langle\mathcal P\rangle\cdot B=\{n:n\text{ reaches }1\}\).

*Proof.* \(\subseteq\): each \(P_e\) satisfies (T), and \(1\) reaches \(1\).
\(\supseteq\): let \(n\) be odd and reach \(1\), with odd-step trajectory
\(n=n_0\to n_1\to\cdots\to n_r=1\), \(n_{j+1}=f(n_j)\),
\(e_j=v_2(3n_j+1)\). Then \(2^{e_j}n_{j+1}=3n_j+1\), so
\(P_{e_j}(n_{j+1})=\frac{2^{e_j}n_{j+1}-1}{3}=n_j\). Applying
\(P_{e_{r-1}},P_{e_{r-2}},\dots,P_{e_0}\) in that order to \(1\in B\)
produces \(n\). Even \(n\) are handled by \(\delta\) on the odd part.
\(\square\)

So the classification problem W2 poses is, for the natural maximal generator
set, *literally* the Collatz conjecture. Any proper generating set the repo
can name restricts the valuation alphabet to a single letter (\(e=2\) for
\(P_2\)/TWR1) or to a sibling rule (\(\sigma\)), and then §4 applies.

The intermediate regime — finitely many valuations \(E\), \(|E|\ge2\) — is
not thin and not the conjecture; it is the bounded-parity-vector predecessor
tree, whose counting function is the subject of the Krasikov–Lagarias /
Applegate–Lagarias literature and of task **W4**. That is where a
quantitative W2 would have to go, and W4 already owns it. W2 is therefore
not merely killed but **absorbed**: its only live descendant is W4.

## 6. Barrier check and honest scope

1. *Finite-state information.* Not invoked. The kill is by counting, not by
   a modular-language argument, so barrier 1 is **not** what closes this.
   (The task predicted it would be; it is not.)
2. *Density / entropy.* §4 is a density statement, but it is used only
   *negatively* — to show a proposed method cannot reach the targets. No
   cylinder is claimed eliminated. Barrier 2 is respected.
3. *Variable-height Diophantine.* Not invoked.
4. *Virtual collisions.* Not applicable: every transport rule here is an
   identity between *actual* trajectories, which is exactly why (T) holds.

**Explicit non-claims.** Nothing here bears on Avenue A, storage dominance,
or the IEF frontier. W2-A is a simplification of TWR1, not a strengthening:
it re-derives T1(a),(b) and the domain condition, and says nothing about
T1(d) (the carry-free periodic normal form) or T1(e) (the \(2\)-adic
separation), which remain TWR1's own content and are still what W8 needs.

## 7. Falsifier

- **W2-A** is falsified by any \((z,d)\) with \(3^d\mid z-1\) and
  \(P_2^d(z)\ne1+(4/3)^d(z-1)\), or by a tower member \(w_d(M)\) not equal to
  \(P_2^d(2^M-1)\). Checked for all odd \(z<4000\), \(d\le7\), and for
  \(d\le5\) with five Mersenne seeds each.
- **W2-B/C** are falsified by exhibiting an element of
  \(\langle\sigma,\delta\rangle\cdot B\) with two distinct representations
  \(2^m\sigma^k(b)\), \(b\in B'\) — this would break the count. The verifier
  cross-checks the analytic count against explicit enumeration at three
  scales.
- **The kill is falsified** by producing a transport rule that is (a) not
  void, (b) self-composable on its own image, and (c) has growth factor
  bounded above as well as below — the last is what would defeat the
  \(\Theta((\log X)^2)\) counting. No such rule is known to this repo, and
  W2-G says the natural way to get one is to admit two valuations, which is
  W4's problem.
